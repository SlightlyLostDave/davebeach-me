Design system — one direction, v1.0

Code, cartography,
and the field notes between.

A visual language for a portfolio that has to read as an engineer's and a surveyor's at once. Everything below comes from a small set of decisions: the colour and type tokens already in global.css, a mono face added as the metadata voice, hairlines instead of shadows, and motion that behaves like an instrument rather than an animation. Five deliberate departures from the shipped site are listed at the end.

01

Principles

P—01

Instrumented, not decorated

Every ornament carries information — a coordinate, a date, a stack, a status. If a mark says nothing, delete it.

P—02

Hairlines, not shadows

Structure comes from 1px rules and flat surfaces, the way a drawing sheet does. No drop shadows, no glass, no glow.

P—03

Mono is the metadata voice

Prose is grotesk. Anything a machine would emit — labels, dates, coords, stacks, statuses — is mono, tracked out, often uppercase.

P—04

Motion reads as measurement

Transitions are short and directional — a sweep, a scan, a snap to grid. No bounce, no float, no parallax drift.

04

Grid, space, edges

12 columns — 24px gutter — 1200 max — 20/40/60px margin

Standard spans: prose 7 columns, small cards 3, project cards 6, timeline body 8 against a 4-column mono gutter. Page margins follow the shipped .c-space ramp (20 / 40 / 60px). Section rhythm becomes 80px vertical padding with a hairline rule between — replacing the current .section-spacing min-height:100vh, which forces air even where a section has little to say.

Motifs

Six devices, all built from CSS gradients and text — no illustration, nothing to maintain. Budget: at most two motifs per screen, one per section. Figures in M—05 and M—06 are generic examples, not project data.

M—01 Contours

Concentric hairlines at 20–46px intervals, 8–30% opacity. Section backgrounds and card headers. Never behind body copy.

32px minor / 128px major grid. The major line is sage at 22%; minor is white at 7%. Use for empty states and map frames.

09

Departures from the shipped site

Colour and type above are the tokens already in global.css. These five are real changes — each one is a proposal, not a description of what exists today.

D—01 Radius
→

Today: rounded-2xl cards, rounded-full buttons, rounded-md inputs. Proposed: a flat 3px everywhere, pills reserved for tags. Soft corners read consumer-app; drawing sheets have square ones.

D—02 Third accent
→

Today: --color-accent #ffd166 is a third hue at higher chroma than either sibling. Proposed: retire it and let secondary carry those moments, so the palette holds one lightness and one chroma.

D—03 Gradient fills
→

Today: .grid-default-color fills about cards with a primary→#748c6e gradient. Proposed: flat surface plus a hairline, accent kept for marks and type. It also fixes contrast — dark text on a shifting green is unpredictable.

D—04 A mono face
2.4 cm/px → 2.4 cm/px

Today: two faces, no mono — every figure and coordinate is set in Raleway. Proposed: add IBM Plex Mono as a third family. It is the single change that does most of the work in the brief, and it costs one @fontsource-variable package.

D—05 Section rhythm
100vh
→
80px

Today: .section-spacing pins every section to min-h-screen. Proposed: 80px padding and a hairline, so a section is exactly as tall as its content — and a thin section reads as a prompt to write more, not as designed emptiness.

Unchanged

Primary, secondary, base-100, and both text colours; Cabin and Raleway; the Tailwind 4px spacing base; .c-space page margins; content driven from cv.json; the GSAP timeline and the Three.js hero — the shader Earth is the site's best asset and the survey-mark motif is built to frame it, not replace it.

10

Rules of thumb

Do
Give every project two numbers only it could claim. Let sage mean geospatial and clay mean field capture, everywhere. Caption every image placeholder with what belongs there. Keep one filled button and one ambient loop per viewport. Separate sections with a hairline and 80px of air.
Don't
Don't run a 24-logo tech wall — name the tools that matter per project. Don't use ramp colors for UI, or accent colors for data. Don't add shadows, glows, or gradient headlines. Don't stack more than two motifs on one screen. Don't let a section exist without something specific to say.
